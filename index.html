Rule one

Your guest is never left standing alone.

A guest alone in a loud room decides one thing: I do not belong here. That decision happens in about 30 seconds and you never get it back.

Your guest is 100% your responsibility.
Alone
"I'll just check my phone."
Escorted
"Come meet somebody."
The move

Edify before you introduce.

You cannot build yourself up. You build up the other person, and they build you up. Credibility gets handed across the circle. Nobody claims it for themselves.

"Olivia is a successful entrepreneur who leads a team of more than 500 people. She is a mom of three, and she taught me how to win in business. I can't believe you get to meet her today."

The introduction

Walk them to Olivia. Build Olivia up first.

Part 01
Set it up as you walk.
"I'm excited to introduce you to our main speaker, Olivia." Then walk them over. Do not park them at the wall.
Part 02
Edify Olivia first.
Tell the guest why Olivia matters: her results, leadership, character, and why you respect her.
Part 03
Give Olivia the short brief.
"Olivia, this is Alex. Alex is a teacher and came tonight to learn about building something alongside school."
80

80% Olivia. 20% guest. Never flip it. Olivia gets the full build-up. The guest gets a fast background and a reason for being here.

Then get out of the way

Let the other person do the telling.

Once you have set it up, you stop talking. A story from a third party lands ten times harder than the same story from you. You are the matchmaker, not the message.

Two mouths talking, one mouth listening. Yours is the one listening.

Who you walk them to

Match them to somebody who looks like their life.

A teacher should meet a teacher. A dad of two should meet a dad of two. People do not join a company. They join a person they can see themselves becoming.

"Let me introduce you to Maya. She teaches too."

Before they leave

Widen the circle. Then stay.

One guest should meet three or four people in one night. Every handshake is another reason to come back. You stay next to them the whole time.

"There are a couple more people I'd love for you to meet."

One guest · four real conversations

Set the room

Your energy becomes their first evidence.

Bring energy
Be excited on purpose.
Your guest reads the room through you. Bring warmth, certainty, and full attention.
Build proof
Capture the room.
Take photos and short videos of real conversations, introductions, and people connecting.
Make it visible
Share the experience.
Use the team's approved event tag and show what a strong room feels like.

High energy is not noise. It is a guest feeling that they are in the right room with the right people.

After the event

Leave with the next get-together, not just a goodbye.

Reconnect while the experience is fresh. Ask what stood out, listen for the real answer, then set the next get-together within 24 hours. Before they leave, introduce them to a trainer who knows what they liked and when you will get together next.

"What stood out to you most tonight?"
